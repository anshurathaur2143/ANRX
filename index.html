HRMS suite for US hospital clients

01 / Business context & analysis approach

The suite combines employee lifecycle workflows with hospital operations and safety tools. My BA focus is to turn policy-heavy needs into requirements that can be reviewed, built, tested and traced. I clarify actors and permissions, trigger events, required data, status transitions, approval ownership, exception paths, reporting needs and integrations before treating a requirement as delivery-ready.

BRD — what & why
  • Business objective, scope, stakeholders, assumptions, constraints and success measures.
  • High-level needs: reduce manual hand-offs, improve visibility, standardize policy execution and maintain auditability.
  • Out-of-scope items and dependencies called out before baseline approval.
FRD — expected system behaviour
  • Actors, preconditions, trigger, main flow, alternate/exception flows and postconditions.
  • Field-level validation, permissions, business rules, notifications, reports and integration behaviour.
  • Requirement IDs linked to stories, acceptance criteria and test coverage through traceability.

02 / Nine modules — functionality and BA logic

01Vendor Management System (VMS)Development ~7 months · new-client deployment 3–5 weeks

Functional flow: vendor onboarding → certification/W-9/NDA and compliance review → contract and SLA setup → performance review → invoice submission → approval/payment → complaint resolution or contract termination.

Rules to specify: mandatory documents by vendor type, expiry alerts, contract effective/renewal dates, role-based access, SLA/KPI thresholds, invoice matching and approval hierarchy, rejected invoice correction, grievance ownership and audit history.

BA deliverables: stakeholder map (vendor, procurement, finance, compliance), as-is/to-be workflow, vendor lifecycle states, field/validation matrix, invoice exception scenarios, approval matrix and UAT cases.

Example acceptance: an invoice cannot move to payment approval until required contract/vendor checks pass; a rejected invoice returns with a reason and remains traceable.

02Applicant Tracking System (ATS)Development ~6 months · new-client deployment 2–4 weeks

Functional flow: department raises job requisition → authorized approval → candidate sourcing/referral → screening and shortlisting → interview panel scheduling → evaluation → offer approval → background checks and digital acceptance.

Rules to specify: requisition budget/role approval, screening criteria, candidate status transitions, duplicate applications, interviewer availability, feedback completion, offer approval thresholds and hand-off to vendor-submitted candidates from VMS.

BA deliverables: role/status matrix, recruitment process map, candidate data fields, calendar and VMS integration requirements, rejection/withdrawal paths and scenario-based acceptance criteria.

Example acceptance: an offer cannot be issued before required approvals and interview feedback are complete; a candidate status change is visible to authorized users.

03Timesheet ManagementDevelopment ~4 months · new-client deployment 2–4 weeks

Functional flow: assign shift → capture login/logout or punch data → calculate worked hours and overtime → employee/manager review → resolve exceptions → approve → pass approved hours to payroll.

Rules to specify: overnight shifts, missed punches, breaks, weekend/holiday rules, overtime thresholds, emergency duty, duplicate entries, correction permissions, escalation when approval is late and FLSA-related policy requirements.

BA deliverables: time calculation examples, exception matrix, shift-state workflow, payroll data mapping, approval SLA and UAT scenarios for normal, boundary and corrected timesheets.

Example acceptance: an overnight shift is attributed to the correct work period and approved overtime is transferred once—not duplicated—to payroll.

04Leave ManagementDevelopment ~3 months · new-client deployment 1–3 weeks

Functional flow: employee selects leave type and dates → system checks eligibility/balance/policy → request routes to approver(s) → approve/reject/return → balance and team calendar update → relevant absence data becomes available to workforce/payroll processes.

Rules to specify: sick/vacation/bereavement/FMLA and other configured leave types, accrual frequency, carry-forward, encashment, overlapping requests, holidays, partial days, cancellations, delegation and multi-level approvals.

BA deliverables: policy-to-rule matrix, leave balance examples, role/approval matrix, calendar/report requirements and edge-case test scenarios.

Example acceptance: a request exceeding an employee’s eligible balance follows the agreed rule and explains the outcome; approval updates the balance and calendar consistently.

05Payroll ManagementDevelopment ~6 months · new-client deployment 4–8 weeks

Functional flow: retrieve approved timesheet/attendance → apply salary structure and shift differentials → calculate taxes, benefits and deductions → validate payroll totals → generate payslip → reconcile and retain an auditable result.

Rules to specify: hourly/salaried pay, overtime inputs, federal/state/local taxes, Medicare, Social Security, 401(k) and other configured deductions, rounding, effective-dated salary changes, missing timesheets, retroactive adjustments and payroll-period cut-offs.

BA deliverables: payroll component/data dictionary, calculation examples, source-to-target mapping, exception handling, reconciliation scenarios, parallel-run checks and UAT sign-off criteria.

Example acceptance: payroll totals reconcile to approved hours and configured pay rules; a missing approval is flagged rather than silently included.

06Learning Management System (LMS)Development ~3.5 months · new-client deployment 2–4 weeks

Functional flow: create or map a course → assign by role/team → employee launches content → quiz/progress is recorded → completion rule is evaluated → certificate is generated → expiry alert/report is produced where applicable.

Rules to specify: role-based access, required versus optional learning, pass score, attempts, completion status, certificate validity/expiry and reminders for compliance training such as HIPAA, BLS or OSHA.

BA deliverables: course lifecycle, permission matrix, completion-state model, notification rules, reporting fields and UAT scenarios for incomplete, failed, passed and expired training.

07Hospital Management System (HMS, SaaS)Core development ~2.5 months · new-client deployment 2–4 weeks

Documented initial scope: patient registration/billing, bed/room management and doctor scheduling. The brief describes EHR and inventory capabilities in the wider product overview; its timeline note says the initial build focused on selected core features.

Rules to specify: required patient/insurance/consent fields, billing estimate rules, bed occupancy states, admission/transfer/discharge transitions, room-cleaning readiness, roster availability, specialty coverage and consecutive duty-hour constraints.

BA deliverables: patient/bed/roster workflow maps, status and permission matrix, billing examples, scheduling constraints, exception paths and QA acceptance scenarios.

08Insurance & Claims Management (SaaS)Development ~5 months · new-client deployment 3–6 weeks

Functional flow: verify eligibility/coverage → capture required claim data → submit claim electronically → track payer status/remittance → classify denial reason → correct data or route for resubmission.

Rules to specify: coverage effective dates, required payer fields, missing authorizations, claim status transitions, duplicate submissions, denial categories, correction ownership and audit trail. The brief references EDI 270/271, 837 and 835 interactions.

BA deliverables: payer/data mapping, status model, validation matrix, denial reason taxonomy, integration error handling and test cases for accepted, rejected, pending and resubmitted claims.

09Infection & Fatigue Management (SaaS)Development ~2 months · new-client deployment 1–2 weeks

Functional flow: collect infection and workforce signals → calculate configured indicators/fatigue risk → compare with thresholds → trigger alerts → route follow-up actions → produce compliance reports.

Rules to specify: indicator definitions, threshold ownership, alert severity, recipient/escalation rules, acknowledgement, repeat-alert suppression, reporting period and audit evidence. The brief references HAI monitoring, CDC/NHSN reporting and duty-hour risk considerations.

BA deliverables: metric/data definitions, alert decision table, dashboard requirements, role-based visibility, compliance report fields and threshold/boundary test cases.

CASE 02 / HMS
PATIENT FLOW · BED STATE · ROSTER

HEALTHCARE · SAAS · HOSPITAL OPERATIONS

Hospital Management System

The initial product scope described in the HRMS brief focused on patient registration and billing, bed/room management, and doctor scheduling. The BA challenge is keeping patient, bed and roster states consistent across hand-offs.

As-is / to-be workflowBusiness rulesGap analysisQA acceptance
Open the full BA case study ↗

01 / Workflow analysis

01Register patient

Capture demographics, insurance, consent and required admission data; validate mandatory fields and possible duplicates.

02Estimate billing

Generate an estimate from configured billing rules and available coverage data; surface missing or unverified information.

03Assign bed / room

Show occupancy and readiness; prevent assignment to unavailable, occupied or cleaning-in-progress beds.

04Schedule doctor

Match availability and specialty to coverage needs; flag conflicts and duty-hour constraints.

05Discharge & release

Record discharge summary and move the bed through cleaning/readiness states before it is offered again.

02 / Business rules & exception paths

  • Patient registration: required demographic, insurance and consent fields must pass validation before the workflow advances.
  • Bed availability: a bed is assignable only when its state is available; occupied, reserved or cleaning states block assignment.
  • Discharge: bed availability must not change to “ready” until required discharge and room-readiness steps are complete.
  • Doctor roster: scheduling must consider specialty, availability, duty hours and critical-care coverage; conflicting assignments require resolution.
  • Billing: estimates should clearly distinguish verified coverage from assumptions or missing payer data.

03 / Gap analysis example

Possible as-is gap to validate

Staff rely on manual bed-status updates, so occupancy visibility may lag behind transfers, discharge or room cleaning.

Proposed to-be behaviour

Use explicit bed states, define the role responsible for each transition, show last-updated status and prevent invalid assignment.

This is a functional analysis example based on the described module scope, not a claim that every hospital used the same legacy process.

04 / QA acceptance scenarios

  • Register a patient with all mandatory fields; verify the record and billing estimate are created.
  • Attempt registration with missing consent or required insurance data; verify the configured validation response.
  • Assign an available bed; then attempt to assign the same bed to a second patient and confirm the conflict is blocked.
  • Discharge a patient; verify the bed follows the configured cleaning/readiness states and is not immediately shown as ready prematurely.
  • Schedule two doctors into overlapping shifts; verify conflict detection and the expected resolution path.
  • Change a patient’s status; confirm downstream occupancy views and reports reflect the same state.

05 / Delivery timeline

~2.5 monthscore product development
2–4 weeksnew-client deployment

The brief attributes the shorter initial build to a focused scope—registration/billing, bed/room management and doctor scheduling—rather than full EHR and inventory complexity. Client rollout varies with bed naming, billing codes, roster rules, configuration, UAT and training.

Resume-reported outcomes: 30% less manual bed-tracking effort and 90%+ first-pass QA acceptance. Keep the baseline and measurement period ready to explain in an interview.

E-COMMERCE · B2B / B2C · INTEGRATIONS

E-commerce platform & integrations

Requirements across product catalog, cart, checkout, payments and order workflows for platforms serving 50,000+ users. The BA challenge is to define the data contract and the behaviour when external services return delayed, invalid or failed responses.

API requirementsSQL validationUATRelease traceability
Open the full BA case study ↗

01 / Business flow

Browse catalog→Cart validation→Checkout→Payment response→Order status

02 / API requirements I would baseline

  • Contract: endpoint purpose, method, required/optional fields, data types, accepted values, authentication, response codes and versioning.
  • Request validation: product/SKU exists, quantity is valid, price/currency are consistent, shipping address is complete and required customer data is present.
  • Success path: accepted payment creates or confirms the order, records a transaction reference and returns a clear status to the client.
  • Failure path: declined payment, timeout, duplicate callback, inventory change, invalid response or unavailable service must produce a defined state and user message.
  • Idempotency: retries must not create duplicate charges or duplicate orders; define correlation IDs and reconciliation expectations where supported.
  • Ownership: identify source of truth for inventory, pricing, payment status and order status; specify retry, timeout, logging and support behaviour.

03 / SQL validation approach

Start from a business question and confirm table definitions. For example, reconcile order header, order items and payment transactions using stable IDs; compare order totals against item totals, discount, tax and shipping; identify duplicate transaction references, orders with no payment record, payments without a completed order, and status mismatches. Validate join cardinality and time windows before reporting totals.

Example validation: one order with two line items should not be double-counted after joining multiple payment events. Aggregate at the correct grain, then reconcile the final total to a trusted report or source.

04 / UAT scenarios

  • Successful checkout with valid stock, address and payment.
  • Payment decline: no false “order confirmed” status; user receives the agreed next step.
  • Payment timeout followed by a retry: no duplicate order/charge; final status can be reconciled.
  • Inventory drops between cart and checkout: quantity/availability is revalidated and the user sees a clear outcome.
  • API returns missing or malformed fields, rate limit or server error: expected handling and logging are defined.
  • Order, item and payment records reconcile correctly for refund/cancellation and status reporting.

05 / Reported impact

The current portfolio summary reports contribution to a 15% reduction in cart abandonment and 12+ feature enhancements. In an interview, connect this to the measured funnel stage, release period and your specific BA contribution rather than implying a single change caused the full result.

CASE 04 / CONTENT AI
DRAFT → REVIEW → PUBLISH
CONTENTAI ASSISTAPPROVAL
AI-ENABLED ELEARNING · DIGITAL PUBLISHING

UMGC AI-based content platform

Requirements for an AI-enabled content platform supporting eLearning material creation and layout automation across 25+ courses. The key BA challenge is to keep AI-assisted edits reviewable, reversible and governed through the content lifecycle.

Requirement gatheringFunctional requirementsWorkflow designReporting
Open the full BA case study ↗

01 / Functional workflow

01Create / import

Author or import course content and capture title, owner, course, version and status.

02AI-assisted edit

Request summarization, grammar correction, text optimization or layout assistance.

03Review changes

Compare proposed output with source, accept or reject changes and preserve an editable version.

04Version & approve

Track revisions, author/reviewer, timestamps and approval status before publication.

05Preview & publish

Validate layout, required content and publishing permissions; record final status and report throughput.

02 / Requirements and business rules

  • Content integrity: preserve the original version so users can compare, reject or recover from AI-assisted changes.
  • Review control: AI-generated text is a proposed change; publication follows the configured human review/approval workflow.
  • Versioning: every saved revision records version, author/action, timestamp and status; define how concurrent edits are handled.
  • Permissions: authors edit their assigned content, reviewers approve or return it, and publishers release only approved versions.
  • Lifecycle: draft → in review → changes requested/approved → published; define allowed transitions and required fields.
  • Reporting: track content volume, time in each status, turnaround time, revision count and publication completion with clear metric definitions.

03 / Acceptance criteria examples

  • When a user requests a summary, the system returns a proposed version without silently overwriting the source.
  • When a reviewer requests changes, the item returns to the author with feedback and retains its review history.
  • Only an authorized role can publish an approved version; draft or returned content cannot bypass required approval.
  • A user can inspect version history and identify who changed content and when.
  • Publishing preview flags missing required fields or layout issues before final publication.

04 / Reporting and impact

The portfolio summary reports 25% faster content turnaround and workflow tracking across 100+ content pieces per month. Be prepared to explain the baseline, how turnaround was measured and which parts of the process you directly influenced.
